## SUMMARY 3.1
In short: Position \(x\) locates an object on an axis measured from an origin you choose. Displacement \(\Delta x = x_f - x_i\) is the change in position and carries a sign; distance traveled is the total path length and is never negative. Average velocity is displacement over elapsed time, so a round trip has zero average velocity even though you moved.
Watch out: Average speed is total distance over elapsed time. It is not the magnitude of the average velocity.

## SUMMARY 3.2
In short: Instantaneous velocity is the derivative of position, \(v = dx/dt\), which is the slope of the tangent line on an \(x(t)\) graph. Instantaneous speed is its magnitude. Where \(x(t)\) has a maximum or minimum, \(v = 0\) and the object turns around.
Watch out: A negative velocity means motion in the negative direction. It does not mean the object is slowing down.

## SUMMARY 3.3
In short: Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity: \(\bar a = \Delta v/\Delta t\) on average and \(a = dv/dt\) at an instant, the slope of the \(v(t)\) graph. An object speeds up when \(a\) and \(v\) have the same sign and slows down when the signs are opposite.
Watch out: Negative acceleration is not the same as slowing down. Compare the signs of \(a\) and \(v\).

## SUMMARY 3.4
In short: With constant acceleration, four equations connect \(\Delta x\), \(v_0\), \(v\), \(a\), and \(t\). Each one leaves out a single quantity, so pick the equation that contains your unknown and leaves out the quantity you neither know nor need. For a pursuit problem, write \(x(t)\) for each object and set the positions equal.
Watch out: Choose a positive direction before you start, and give every vector quantity its sign.

## SUMMARY 3.5
In short: Ignoring air resistance, every object near Earth's surface falls with the same constant acceleration of magnitude \(g = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\). With up as positive, \(a = -g\) and the constant acceleration equations apply with \(y\) in place of \(x\). At the top of a vertical toss \(v = 0\), but the acceleration is still \(-g\).
Watch out: \(g\) itself is positive. The sign belongs in \(a = -g\), so don't write \(g = -9.8\) and then also subtract it.

## SUMMARY 3.6
In short: When acceleration changes with time, integrate: \(v(t) = \int a(t)\,dt + C_1\) and \(x(t) = \int v(t)\,dt + C_2\). The initial velocity and position fix the constants. The constant acceleration equations are the special case \(a(t) = a\).
Watch out: Don't use the constant acceleration equations when \(a\) depends on time.

# ============================ 3.1 POSITION AND AVERAGE VELOCITY ============================

## QUIZ 3.1
Q: A runner completes one 400 m lap of a track in 80 s and stops where she started. What is her average velocity for the lap?
A) 5.0 m/s
B) 2.5 m/s
C) 0 m/s
D) It depends on the direction she ran
Answer: C
Why: Average velocity is displacement over time. She ends where she started, so \(\Delta x = 0\) and \(\bar v = 0\). Her average speed is 5.0 m/s.

## QUIZ 3.1
Q: How does average speed compare with the magnitude of average velocity over the same trip?
A) Always equal
B) Always greater than or equal
C) Always less than or equal
D) Either can be larger
Answer: B
Why: Distance traveled is at least as large as the magnitude of the displacement, so average speed is at least \(|\bar v|\). They are equal only when the motion never reverses.

## QUIZ 3.1
Q: Which of these is a vector quantity?
A) Distance traveled
B) Displacement
C) Speed
D) Elapsed time
Answer: B
Why: Displacement has a direction, shown by its sign in one dimension. Distance, speed, and time have size only.

## QUIZ 3.1
Q: Can the distance traveled ever be less than the magnitude of the displacement?
A) Yes, when the object moves in the negative direction
B) Yes, when the object turns around
C) No, never
D) Only when the object speeds up
Answer: C
Why: The straight line change in position is the shortest possible path, so distance traveled is always at least \(|\Delta x|\).

# ============================ 3.2 INSTANTANEOUS VELOCITY ============================

## QUIZ 3.2
Q: On a graph of position versus time, what gives the instantaneous velocity at a moment?
A) The height of the curve at that moment
B) The area under the curve up to that moment
C) The slope of the tangent line at that moment
D) The curvature at that moment
Answer: C
Why: \(v = dx/dt\), which is the slope of the tangent line to \(x(t)\).

## QUIZ 3.2
Q: On a position versus time graph, what gives the average velocity between two times?
A) The slope of the straight line connecting the two points
B) The slope of the tangent line at the later time
C) The area under the curve between the two times
D) The average of the two heights
Answer: A
Why: \(\bar v = \Delta x/\Delta t\), which is the slope of the straight line (the secant) between the two points.

## QUIZ 3.2
Q: A car's speedometer shows which quantity?
A) Instantaneous velocity
B) Instantaneous speed
C) Average velocity
D) Average speed
Answer: B
Why: It shows how fast you're going right now, with no direction. That's the magnitude of the instantaneous velocity.

## QUIZ 3.2
Q: An object has a velocity of \(-3\ \text{m/s}\). Which must be true?
A) It is slowing down
B) It is moving in the negative direction
C) Its acceleration is negative
D) It is at a negative position
Answer: B
Why: The sign of velocity tells the direction of motion, nothing more. It could be speeding up or slowing down, anywhere on the axis.

# ============================ 3.3 ACCELERATION ============================

## QUIZ 3.3
Q: A car moving in the negative \(x\) direction is slowing down. What is the sign of its acceleration?
A) Negative
B) Positive
C) Zero
D) It depends on the speed
Answer: B
Why: Slowing down means the acceleration points opposite to the velocity. The velocity is negative, so the acceleration is positive.

## QUIZ 3.3
Q: A velocity versus time graph is a horizontal line above the time axis. What does that describe?
A) Constant positive velocity, zero acceleration
B) Constant positive acceleration
C) An object at rest
D) Speeding up in the positive direction
Answer: A
Why: The velocity doesn't change, so the slope of \(v(t)\), the acceleration, is zero.

## QUIZ 3.3
Q: An object has a negative velocity and a negative acceleration. What is it doing?
A) Speeding up
B) Slowing down
C) Staying at rest
D) Moving at constant speed
Answer: A
Why: When velocity and acceleration have the same sign, the object speeds up, no matter which sign it is.

## QUIZ 3.3
Q: Can an object have zero velocity and nonzero acceleration at the same moment?
A) No, zero velocity means zero acceleration
B) Yes, for example a ball at the top of its flight
C) Only far from Earth
D) Only if it is speeding up
Answer: B
Why: At the top of a toss \(v = 0\) for an instant, but its velocity is still changing, so \(a = -g\).

## QUIZ 3.4
Q: You know \(v_0\), \(a\), and \(\Delta x\), and you want \(v\). The time isn't given. Which equation goes straight to the answer?
A) \(v = v_0 + at\)
B) \(\Delta x = v_0t + \tfrac12at^2\)
C) \(v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x\)
D) \(\Delta x = \tfrac12(v_0+v)t\)
Answer: C
Why: It is the only constant acceleration equation without \(t\).

## QUIZ 3.4
Q: You know \(\Delta x\), \(v_0\), and \(t\), and you want \(a\). Which equation goes straight to the answer?
A) \(v = v_0 + at\)
B) \(\Delta x = v_0t + \tfrac12at^2\)
C) \(v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a\Delta x\)
D) \(\Delta x = \tfrac12(v_0+v)t\)
Answer: B
Why: It contains \(\Delta x\), \(v_0\), \(t\), and \(a\), and leaves out the final velocity you don't know.

## QUIZ 3.4
Q: An object starts from rest with constant acceleration and travels 4.0 m in the first 2.0 s. How far has it traveled after 4.0 s?
A) 8.0 m
B) 12 m
C) 16 m
D) 32 m
Answer: C
Why: From rest, \(x = \tfrac12at^2\), so doubling the time multiplies the distance by 4: \(4 \times 4.0\ \text{m} = 16\ \text{m}\).

## QUIZ 3.4
Q: With constant acceleration, the average velocity over a time interval equals what?
A) \((v_0 + v)/2\)
B) \(v/2\), always
C) \(v_0 + at\)
D) \(\Delta x \cdot t\)
Answer: A
Why: Velocity changes at a steady rate, so its average is halfway between the start and end values. \(v/2\) only works when starting from rest.

## QUIZ 3.4
Q: Car 1 starts from rest and speeds up just as car 2 passes it at constant speed. When car 1 catches up, how do their speeds compare?
A) They are equal
B) Car 1 is faster
C) Car 2 is faster
D) It depends on the acceleration
Answer: B
Why: They cover the same distance in the same time, so their average velocities match. Car 1's average is half its final speed, so its final speed is twice car 2's.

# ============================ 3.5 FREE FALL ============================

## QUIZ 3.5
Q: A ball is thrown straight up. At the very top of its path, which is true?
A) \(v = 0\) and \(a = 0\)
B) \(v = 0\) and \(a = 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2\) downward
C) \(v\) is largest and \(a = 0\)
D) \(v = 0\) and \(a\) points up
Answer: B
Why: The velocity is momentarily zero, but gravity still acts. The acceleration is \(g\) downward the whole time.

## QUIZ 3.5
Q: From the same height, ball A is dropped and ball B is thrown straight down at 5 m/s. Ignoring air resistance, how do their accelerations compare?
A) A's is larger
B) B's is larger
C) They are equal
D) B's is larger at first, then they are equal
Answer: C
Why: Both are in free fall, so both accelerate at \(g\). The initial velocity doesn't change the acceleration.

## QUIZ 3.5
Q: With up as positive, what is the acceleration of a ball while it is still rising?
A) \(+g\)
B) \(-g\)
C) Zero
D) \(+g\) going up, then \(-g\) coming down
Answer: B
Why: Gravity pulls down the whole time, so \(a = -g\) on the way up, at the top, and on the way down.

## QUIZ 3.5
Q: A ball is thrown straight up and caught at the same height. Ignoring air resistance, how does its speed when caught compare with its launch speed?
A) The same
B) Smaller
C) Larger
D) It depends on the ball's mass
Answer: A
Why: The motion is symmetric. The ball loses speed at \(g\) going up and gains it back at \(g\) coming down, so it returns with the same speed, moving downward.

## QUIZ 3.5
Q: Ignoring air resistance, a bowling ball and a marble are dropped together from the same height. Which lands first?
A) The bowling ball
B) The marble
C) They land together
D) It depends on the height
Answer: C
Why: All objects in free fall have the same acceleration \(g\), regardless of mass.

# ============================ 3.6 VELOCITY AND DISPLACEMENT FROM ACCELERATION ============================

## QUIZ 3.6
Q: A particle starts from rest at \(t = 0\) with \(a(t) = (6\ \text{m/s}^3)\,t\). What is its velocity at \(t = 2.0\ \text{s}\)?
A) 6.0 m/s
B) 12 m/s
C) 24 m/s
D) 3.0 m/s
Answer: B
Why: \(v(t) = \int 6t\,dt + C_1 = 3t^2\) with \(C_1 = 0\) since it starts from rest. At 2.0 s, \(v = 3(2.0)^2 = 12\ \text{m/s}\).

## QUIZ 3.6
Q: You know \(a(t)\). What else do you need to find the position \(x(t)\)?
A) The initial velocity and initial position
B) Only the final velocity
C) Nothing else
D) The object's mass
Answer: A
Why: Each integration adds a constant. The initial velocity fixes the one in \(v(t)\), and the initial position fixes the one in \(x(t)\).

## QUIZ 3.6
Q: The area under an acceleration versus time graph between two times gives what?
A) The change in velocity
B) The change in position
C) The average acceleration
D) The final position
Answer: A
Why: \(\Delta v = \int a\,dt\), and an integral is the area under the curve.

## QUIZ 3.6
Q: The area under a velocity versus time graph between two times gives what?
A) The acceleration
B) The displacement
C) The average velocity
D) The final velocity
Answer: B
Why: \(\Delta x = \int v\,dt\), the area under \(v(t)\). Area below the time axis counts as negative displacement.

## DRILL 3.6
