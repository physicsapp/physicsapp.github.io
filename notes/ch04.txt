## SUMMARY 4.1
In short: In two or three dimensions, position is a vector \(\vec r(t) = x(t)\hat i + y(t)\hat j + z(t)\hat k\). Displacement is \(\Delta\vec r = \vec r(t_2) - \vec r(t_1)\), and velocity is its derivative, \(\vec v = d\vec r/dt\), taken one component at a time: \(v_x = dx/dt\), \(v_y = dy/dt\). The velocity vector is always tangent to the path. Motion along one axis is independent of motion along a perpendicular axis.
Watch out: Speed is the magnitude \(\sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2}\), not \(v_x + v_y\).

## SUMMARY 4.2
In short: Acceleration is the derivative of velocity, \(\vec a = d\vec v/dt\), again one component at a time. It can point in any direction, not just along the velocity. With constant acceleration, the one dimensional equations from Chapter 3 hold separately for \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\), for example \(v_x = v_{0x} + a_xt\) and \(v_y = v_{0y} + a_yt\).
Watch out: An object can accelerate without changing speed, just by changing direction.

## SUMMARY 4.3
In short: A projectile moves only under gravity, so \(a_x = 0\) and \(a_y = -g\). Split the launch velocity into \(v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta_0\) and \(v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta_0\), solve the vertical motion to get the time, then use \(x = v_{0x}t\). Launched and landing at the same height, the time of flight is \(T = 2v_0\sin\theta_0/g\) and the range is \(R = v_0^2\sin 2\theta_0/g\), largest at \(45^\circ\).
Watch out: At the top of the path \(v_y = 0\), but \(v_x\) is not zero and the acceleration is still \(g\) downward.

## SUMMARY 4.4
In short: In uniform circular motion the speed is constant, but the direction keeps changing, so there is a centripetal acceleration \(a_c = v^2/r\) pointing toward the center. If the speed also changes, there is a tangential acceleration \(a_T = d|\vec v|/dt\) along the path. The two are perpendicular, so the total acceleration is \(a = \sqrt{a_c^2 + a_T^2}\).
Watch out: Centripetal acceleration points toward the center, not outward. Nothing pushes a riding passenger outward; they just tend to keep going straight.

## SUMMARY 4.5
In short: Velocity depends on who is measuring. If frame \(S'\) moves with velocity \(\vec v_{S'S}\) relative to frame \(S\), an object's velocity relative to \(S\) is \(\vec v_{PS} = \vec v_{PS'} + \vec v_{S'S}\). The subscripts chain like dominoes: the inner letters match and drop out. Frames moving at constant velocity relative to each other measure the same acceleration.
Watch out: \(\vec v_{AB} = -\vec v_{BA}\). Reversing the subscripts reverses the vector.

## EQUATIONS 4.1
Position vector: \(\vec{r}(t) = x(t)\hat{i} + y(t)\hat{j} + z(t)\hat{k}\) | \(x(t)\), \(y(t)\), \(z(t)\) are the coordinates in meters at time \(t\). The position vector points from the origin to the particle.
Displacement vector: \(\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r}(t_2) - \vec{r}(t_1)\) | \(\vec{r}(t_1)\) and \(\vec{r}(t_2)\) are the positions (m) at two times. The displacement points from the first position to the second.
Velocity vector: \(\vec{v}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\vec{r}(t + \Delta t) - \vec{r}(t)}{\Delta t} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}\) | \(\vec{r}(t)\) is the position in meters and \(t\) the time in seconds, so \(\vec{v}\) is in m/s. The velocity is the derivative of position and is always tangent to the path.
Velocity in components: \(\vec{v}(t) = v_x(t)\hat{i} + v_y(t)\hat{j} + v_z(t)\hat{k}\) | \(v_x\), \(v_y\), \(v_z\) are the velocity components in m/s. The velocity vector is the sum of its x, y and z parts.
Velocity components: \(v_x = \frac{dx}{dt}, \quad v_y = \frac{dy}{dt}, \quad v_z = \frac{dz}{dt}\) | \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) are the coordinates in meters as functions of time. Each velocity component is the derivative of its own coordinate, so each direction can be handled separately.
Average velocity: \(\vec{v}_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\vec{r}(t_2) - \vec{r}(t_1)}{t_2 - t_1}\) | \(\vec{r}(t_1)\) and \(\vec{r}(t_2)\) are the positions (m) and \(t_2 - t_1\) the elapsed time (s). It is the displacement vector divided by time, in m/s.

## EQUATIONS 4.2
Acceleration vector: \(\vec{a}(t) = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\vec{v}(t + \Delta t) - \vec{v}(t)}{\Delta t} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}\) | \(\vec{v}(t)\) is the velocity in m/s and \(t\) the time in seconds, so \(\vec{a}\) is in m/s². Acceleration is any change in velocity, in size or in direction.
Acceleration in components: \(\begin{aligned} \vec{a}(t) &= \frac{dv_x}{dt}\hat{i} + \frac{dv_y}{dt}\hat{j} + \frac{dv_z}{dt}\hat{k} \\ &= a_x\hat{i} + a_y\hat{j} + a_z\hat{k} \end{aligned}\) | \(a_x\), \(a_y\), \(a_z\) are the acceleration components in m/s². Each one is the derivative of the matching velocity component.
Acceleration as a second derivative: \(\vec{a}(t) = \frac{d^2x}{dt^2}\hat{i} + \frac{d^2y}{dt^2}\hat{j} + \frac{d^2z}{dt^2}\hat{k}\) | \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) are the coordinates in meters as functions of time. Differentiating each coordinate twice gives the acceleration components in m/s².

## EQUATIONS 4.3
* Launch velocity components: \(v_{0x} = v_0\cos\theta_0, \quad v_{0y} = v_0\sin\theta_0\) | \(v_0\) is the launch speed (m/s) and \(\theta_0\) the angle above the horizontal. Cosine gives the horizontal part and sine the vertical part.
* Horizontal motion: \(x = x_0 + v_{0x}t\) | \(x_0\) is the starting position (m), \(v_{0x}\) the horizontal velocity (m/s) and \(t\) the time (s). With no air resistance there is no horizontal acceleration, so \(v_x\) stays constant.
* Vertical motion: \(\begin{aligned} y &= y_0 + v_{0y}t - \tfrac{1}{2}gt^2 \\ v_y &= v_{0y} - gt \end{aligned}\) | \(y_0\) is the starting height (m), \(v_{0y}\) the initial vertical velocity (m/s), \(g = 9.8\) m/s² and \(t\) the time (s). Vertically, a projectile is in free fall, independent of its horizontal motion.
* Time of flight (level ground): \(T_{\text{tof}} = \frac{2v_0\sin\theta_0}{g}\) | \(v_0\) is the launch speed (m/s), \(\theta_0\) the launch angle and \(g = 9.8\) m/s², giving the time in seconds. It only works when the projectile lands at the same height it was launched from.
* Trajectory: \(y = (\tan\theta_0)x - \frac{g}{2(v_0\cos\theta_0)^2}x^2\) | \(x\) and \(y\) are the horizontal and vertical positions (m) measured from the launch point, \(v_0\) the launch speed (m/s) and \(\theta_0\) the angle. It shows the path is a parabola.
* Range (level ground): \(R = \frac{v_0^2\sin 2\theta_0}{g}\) | \(v_0\) is the launch speed (m/s), \(\theta_0\) the launch angle and \(g = 9.8\) m/s², giving the range in meters. Only for landing at launch height; the range is largest at 45°.

## EQUATIONS 4.4
* Centripetal acceleration: \(a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}\) | \(v\) is the speed (m/s) and \(r\) the radius of the circle (m), so \(a_c\) is in m/s². It points toward the center of the circle, even when the speed stays constant.
* Speed and period: \(v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}\) | \(r\) is the radius (m) and \(T\) the period, the time for one full turn (s). In one period the object travels one circumference, \(2\pi r\).
Position, uniform circular motion: \(\vec{r}(t) = A\cos\omega t\,\hat{i} + A\sin\omega t\,\hat{j}\) | \(A\) is the radius (m), \(\omega\) the angular frequency (rad/s) and \(t\) the time (s). The particle goes around a circle centered on the origin.
Velocity, uniform circular motion: \(\vec{v}(t) = -A\omega\sin\omega t\,\hat{i} + A\omega\cos\omega t\,\hat{j}\) | \(A\) is the radius (m) and \(\omega\) the angular frequency (rad/s). The velocity has constant size \(A\omega\) and is always tangent to the circle.
Acceleration, uniform circular motion: \(\vec{a}(t) = -A\omega^2\cos\omega t\,\hat{i} - A\omega^2\sin\omega t\,\hat{j}\) | \(A\) is the radius (m) and \(\omega\) the angular frequency (rad/s). The acceleration has size \(A\omega^2\) and points toward the center, opposite to the position vector.
Tangential acceleration: \(a_T = \frac{d|\vec{v}|}{dt}\) | \(|\vec{v}|\) is the speed (m/s) and \(t\) the time (s), so \(a_T\) is in m/s². It changes the speed and points along the path.
Total acceleration: \(\vec{a} = \vec{a}_c + \vec{a}_T\) | \(\vec{a}_c\) points toward the center and changes the direction; \(\vec{a}_T\) points along the path and changes the speed. Together they make the total acceleration in m/s².

## EQUATIONS 4.5
Relative position: \(\vec{r}_{PS} = \vec{r}_{PS'} + \vec{r}_{S'S}\) | \(\vec{r}_{PS}\) is the position of particle P seen from frame S, \(\vec{r}_{PS'}\) seen from frame S', and \(\vec{r}_{S'S}\) the position of S' seen from S, all in meters. Positions add when you chain frames together.
Relative velocity: \(\vec{v}_{PS} = \vec{v}_{PS'} + \vec{v}_{S'S}\) | \(\vec{v}_{PS'}\) is P's velocity seen from S' and \(\vec{v}_{S'S}\) is the velocity of S' seen from S, in m/s. Add them as vectors to get P's velocity seen from S.
Relative velocity, more than two frames: \(\vec{v}_{PC} = \vec{v}_{PA} + \vec{v}_{AB} + \vec{v}_{BC}\) | Each velocity is in m/s, with subscripts read as \"this relative to that\". The inner letters cancel like a chain: P to A, A to B, B to C gives P to C.
Relative acceleration: \(\vec{a}_{PS} = \vec{a}_{PS'} + \vec{a}_{S'S}\) | \(\vec{a}_{S'S}\) is the acceleration of frame S' seen from S, in m/s². If S' moves at constant velocity, both frames measure the same acceleration.

# ============================ 4.1 DISPLACEMENT AND VELOCITY VECTORS ============================

## QUIZ 4.1
Q: How does the velocity vector relate to a particle's path?
A) It points toward the center of the path
B) It is tangent to the path
C) It is perpendicular to the path
D) It points back to the origin
Answer: B
Why: The velocity points in the direction of motion at that instant, which is along the tangent to the path.

## QUIZ 4.1
Q: A car drives once around a circular track and stops where it started. What is its displacement?
A) The circumference of the track
B) The diameter of the track
C) Zero
D) Twice the radius
Answer: C
Why: Displacement depends only on where you start and end. Ending at the start means \(\Delta\vec r = 0\).

## QUIZ 4.1
Q: An object's \(x\) motion and \(y\) motion are:
A) Always the same
B) Independent of each other
C) Always opposite
D) Connected so that \(v_x = v_y\)
Answer: B
Why: Motion along perpendicular axes is independent. You can treat each one with its own one dimensional equations.

## QUIZ 4.1
Q: A particle has \(v_x = 3\ \text{m/s}\) and \(v_y = 4\ \text{m/s}\). What is its speed?
A) 7 m/s
B) 5 m/s
C) 1 m/s
D) 12 m/s
Answer: B
Why: Speed is the magnitude of the velocity, \(\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\ \text{m/s}\).

# ============================ 4.2 ACCELERATION VECTOR ============================

## QUIZ 4.2
Q: A car drives around a curve at a constant 20 m/s. Is it accelerating?
A) No, its speed is constant
B) Yes, its direction of motion is changing
C) Only if it brakes
D) Only on a banked curve
Answer: B
Why: Acceleration is any change in the velocity vector. Changing direction at constant speed is still acceleration.

## QUIZ 4.2
Q: Must the acceleration vector point in the direction of motion?
A) Yes, always
B) No, it can point in any direction
C) Yes, unless the object is slowing down
D) Only in one dimension
Answer: B
Why: Acceleration points the way the velocity is changing. For a thrown ball it points straight down while the ball moves sideways and up.

## QUIZ 4.2
Q: An object has constant acceleration \(\vec a = (2\hat i)\ \text{m/s}^2\). What happens to \(v_y\)?
A) It increases by 2 m/s each second
B) It stays constant
C) It becomes zero
D) It decreases
Answer: B
Why: \(a_y = 0\), so the \(y\) component of velocity doesn't change. Only \(v_x\) changes.

## QUIZ 4.2
Q: Velocity is \(\vec v(t) = (5\hat i + 3t^2\hat j)\ \text{m/s}\). In which direction does the acceleration point?
A) \(+\hat i\)
B) \(+\hat j\), for \(t > 0\)
C) Along the velocity
D) There is no acceleration
Answer: B
Why: \(\vec a = d\vec v/dt = 6t\,\hat j\). The \(x\) component of velocity is constant, so \(a_x = 0\).

## DRILL 4.2

## DRILL 4.2
Q: A puck starts with \(v_{0x} = {{v0}}\ \text{m/s}\) and has constant \(a_x = {{a}}\ \text{m/s}^2\). What is \(v_x\) after \({{t}}\ \text{s}\)?

# ============================ 4.3 PROJECTILE MOTION ============================

## QUIZ 4.3
Q: A ball rolls off a table at the same moment an identical ball is dropped from the same height. Ignoring air resistance, which lands first?
A) The dropped ball
B) The ball that rolled off
C) They land at the same time
D) It depends on how fast the first ball was rolling
Answer: C
Why: Both start with \(v_y = 0\) and fall with the same \(a_y = -g\). Horizontal motion doesn't affect the vertical motion.

## QUIZ 4.3
Q: At the highest point of a projectile's path, what is its velocity?
A) Zero
B) Purely horizontal, equal to \(v_0\cos\theta_0\)
C) Purely vertical
D) Equal to \(v_0\)
Answer: B
Why: \(v_y = 0\) at the top, but \(v_x\) stays \(v_0\cos\theta_0\) the whole flight.

## QUIZ 4.3
Q: Ignoring air resistance, what is a projectile's acceleration while it is moving up?
A) Upward, \(g\)
B) Downward, \(g\)
C) Zero
D) Along the velocity
Answer: B
Why: The only force is gravity, so the acceleration is \(g\) straight down at every point of the flight.

## QUIZ 4.3
Q: On level ground, which launch angle gives the longest range?
A) \(30^\circ\)
B) \(45^\circ\)
C) \(60^\circ\)
D) \(90^\circ\)
Answer: B
Why: \(R = v_0^2\sin 2\theta_0/g\) is largest when \(\sin 2\theta_0 = 1\), so \(\theta_0 = 45^\circ\).

## QUIZ 4.3
Q: On level ground, a ball launched at \(30^\circ\) lands 40 m away. Where does an identical launch at \(60^\circ\) land?
A) 20 m
B) 40 m
C) 80 m
D) 69 m
Answer: B
Why: \(\sin 2\theta_0\) is the same for \(30^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\), so the ranges match. The \(60^\circ\) shot goes higher and stays up longer.

## QUIZ 4.3
Q: A package is dropped from a plane flying level at constant velocity. Ignoring air resistance, where is the package relative to the plane as it falls?
A) Behind the plane
B) Ahead of the plane
C) Directly below the plane
D) It depends on the plane's speed
Answer: C
Why: The package keeps the plane's horizontal velocity, so it stays under the plane while it falls.

## DRILL 4.3

# ============================ 4.4 CIRCULAR MOTION ============================

## QUIZ 4.4
Q: In uniform circular motion, which way does the acceleration point?
A) Along the velocity
B) Toward the center of the circle
C) Away from the center
D) There is no acceleration
Answer: B
Why: The velocity keeps turning toward the center, so the acceleration, \(a_c = v^2/r\), points toward the center.

## QUIZ 4.4
Q: A car on a circular track doubles its speed. What happens to its centripetal acceleration?
A) It doubles
B) It quadruples
C) It halves
D) It stays the same
Answer: B
Why: \(a_c = v^2/r\), so doubling \(v\) multiplies \(a_c\) by 4.

## QUIZ 4.4
Q: A car speeds up while going around a curve. Which accelerations does it have?
A) Only centripetal
B) Only tangential
C) Both centripetal and tangential
D) Neither
Answer: C
Why: The turning gives a centripetal part toward the center, and the change in speed gives a tangential part along the path.

## QUIZ 4.4
Q: At the same speed, which curve gives the larger centripetal acceleration?
A) The one with the larger radius
B) The one with the smaller radius
C) They are equal
D) It depends on the car's mass
Answer: B
Why: \(a_c = v^2/r\), so a tighter curve, smaller \(r\), means a larger acceleration.

## DRILL 4.4

# ============================ 4.5 RELATIVE MOTION ============================

## QUIZ 4.5
Q: You walk toward the front of a train at 1.5 m/s while the train moves at 20 m/s relative to the ground. What is your velocity relative to the ground?
A) 18.5 m/s
B) 20 m/s
C) 21.5 m/s
D) 1.5 m/s
Answer: C
Why: \(\vec v_{you,ground} = \vec v_{you,train} + \vec v_{train,ground} = 1.5 + 20 = 21.5\ \text{m/s}\), in the train's direction.

## QUIZ 4.5
Q: The velocity of A relative to B is \(\vec v_{AB}\). What is \(\vec v_{BA}\)?
A) \(\vec v_{AB}\)
B) \(-\vec v_{AB}\)
C) Zero
D) \(2\vec v_{AB}\)
Answer: B
Why: If A moves east relative to B, then B moves west relative to A at the same speed.

## QUIZ 4.5
Q: Two frames move at constant velocity relative to each other. What do observers in both frames agree on for a moving object?
A) Its velocity
B) Its position
C) Its acceleration
D) Nothing
Answer: C
Why: The relative velocity of the frames is constant, so its derivative is zero, and both frames measure the same acceleration.

## QUIZ 4.5
Q: A boat points straight across a river while the current flows downstream. Relative to the shore, where does the boat end up?
A) Directly across
B) Downstream of directly across
C) Upstream of directly across
D) It never reaches the other side
Answer: B
Why: Relative to the shore, the boat's velocity is its velocity relative to the water plus the water's velocity, so it drifts downstream as it crosses.
