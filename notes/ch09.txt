## SUMMARY 9.1
In short: Momentum is mass times velocity, \(\vec p = m\vec v\), measured in \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\). It is a vector that points the same way as the velocity, so changing direction changes momentum even at constant speed. A heavy, slow object can have as much momentum as a light, fast one. Newton's second law can be written with momentum: the net force is how fast momentum changes, \(\vec F_\text{net} = \frac{d\vec p}{dt}\). With no net force, momentum stays constant.
Watch out: Momentum is not kinetic energy. Momentum grows with \(v\) and has a direction; kinetic energy grows with \(v^2\) and has none.

## SUMMARY 9.2
In short: Impulse is the effect of a force acting over a time: \(\vec J = \int\vec F\,dt\), the area under a force against time graph. For a steady or average force, \(\vec J = \vec F_\text{ave}\Delta t\). The impulse momentum theorem says the impulse equals the change in momentum, \(\vec J = \Delta\vec p\). So the same change in momentum can come from a big force for a short time or a small force for a long time. Airbags, padded floors, and bending your knees when you land all stretch out the stopping time to shrink the force.
Watch out: A bounce needs a bigger impulse than a stop. A ball that hits a wall at \(v\) and comes back at \(v\) has \(|\Delta p| = 2mv\), not \(mv\).

## SUMMARY 9.3
In short: When the net external force on a system is zero, its total momentum stays constant: \(\vec p_1 + \vec p_2 = \text{constant}\). Forces between objects inside the system come in Newton's third law pairs, so they change each object's momentum by equal and opposite amounts and cancel in the total. In a recoil or push apart starting from rest, the total stays zero: \(m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = 0\), so the lighter object moves faster. Momentum can also be conserved in just one direction, for example horizontally when the only outside forces (gravity and the normal force) are vertical.
Watch out: Pick the system so the big forces are inside it. A ball alone is not a closed system when it hits a wall; you need a system with no outside push in the direction you care about.

## SUMMARY 9.4
In short: Momentum is conserved in every collision of a closed system, but kinetic energy is not always. In an elastic collision kinetic energy is conserved too (hard balls, like billiard balls, come close). In an inelastic collision some kinetic energy turns into thermal energy, sound, or bent metal. In a perfectly inelastic collision the objects stick together and share one final velocity, \(v_f = \frac{m_1v_1 + m_2v_2}{m_1 + m_2}\). When two equal masses collide head on and elastically with one at rest, they trade velocities: the moving one stops and the other leaves with its speed.
Watch out: Never conserve kinetic energy in a collision where the objects stick. Use momentum to get the final speed, then compare kinetic energies to see how much was lost.

## SUMMARY 9.5
In short: In two dimensions, momentum is conserved separately in each direction: total \(p_x\) before equals total \(p_x\) after, and the same for \(p_y\). Break every momentum into components, conserve each, then combine with the Pythagorean theorem and \(\tan\theta = \frac{p_y}{p_x}\). When two equal masses collide elastically and one starts at rest, they leave at right angles to each other, \(\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ\). That is why a pool ball and the cue ball split at 90°.
Watch out: Add momentum components, not magnitudes. Two momenta of \(3\) and \(4\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\) at right angles add up to \(5\), not \(7\).

## SUMMARY 9.6
In short: The center of mass is the mass weighted average position: \(\vec r_\text{CM} = \frac{1}{M}\sum m_j\vec r_j\). For two objects it sits on the line between them, closer to the heavier one. Its velocity is the total momentum divided by the total mass, \(\vec v_\text{CM} = \frac{\vec p_\text{tot}}{M}\). Only external forces move it: \(\vec F_\text{ext} = M\vec a_\text{CM}\). A firework that bursts in midair has pieces flying everywhere, but their center of mass keeps following the same parabola.
Watch out: The center of mass does not have to be inside the object. For a ring or a boomerang it is in the empty middle.

## SUMMARY 9.7
In short: A rocket speeds up by throwing mass backward. The exhaust leaves at speed \(u\) relative to the rocket, and by conservation of momentum the rocket gains speed forward; it needs no air to push on. As fuel burns the rocket gets lighter, which leads to the rocket equation \(\Delta v = u\ln\frac{m_i}{m}\). The speed gained depends only on the exhaust speed and the ratio of starting mass to final mass, not on how fast the fuel burns.
Watch out: The ratio inside the logarithm is starting mass over final mass, which is bigger than 1, so \(\Delta v\) comes out positive. A rocket reaches \(\Delta v = u\) when its mass drops by a factor of \(e \approx 2.72\).

## EQUATIONS 9.1
* Momentum: \(\vec p = m\vec v\) | Momentum \(p\) (kg·m/s) is mass \(m\) (kg) times velocity \(v\) (m/s). It points the same way as the velocity.
* Newton's second law with momentum: \(\vec F_\text{net} = \frac{d\vec p}{dt}\) | The net force \(F\) (N) is how fast the momentum changes. For a constant mass it is the same as \(F = ma\).

## EQUATIONS 9.2
* Impulse: \(\vec J = \int_{t_i}^{t_f}\vec F\,dt = \vec F_\text{ave}\Delta t\) | Impulse \(J\) (N·s) is the area under a force against time graph. For a steady or average force it is force times the contact time.
* Impulse momentum theorem: \(\vec J = \Delta\vec p\) | The impulse on an object equals its change in momentum. A longer contact time gives the same change with a smaller force.
Average force from momentum: \(\vec F_\text{ave} = \frac{\Delta\vec p}{\Delta t}\) | Divide the change in momentum (kg·m/s) by the contact time \(\Delta t\) (s) to get the average force (N).

## EQUATIONS 9.3
* Conservation of momentum: \(\vec p_1 + \vec p_2 = \text{constant}\) | With no net external force, the total momentum of a system stays the same. Forces between the objects cancel in pairs.
Conservation for many objects: \(\sum_j \vec p_j = \text{constant}\) | The same rule for any number of objects, as long as the net external force on the whole system is zero.
Two objects in one dimension: \(m_1v_{1,i} + m_2v_{2,i} = m_1v_{1,f} + m_2v_{2,f}\) | Use signs for direction. This holds for every collision, push apart, or explosion between the two.

## EQUATIONS 9.4
* Perfectly inelastic collision: \(v_f = \dfrac{m_1v_1 + m_2v_2}{m_1 + m_2}\) | When the objects stick together they share one final velocity \(v_f\) (m/s). Kinetic energy is lost.
* Elastic collision: \(\begin{aligned}&\tfrac12m_1v_{1,i}^2 + \tfrac12m_2v_{2,i}^2\\ &= \tfrac12m_1v_{1,f}^2 + \tfrac12m_2v_{2,f}^2\end{aligned}\) | In an elastic collision kinetic energy (J) is conserved as well as momentum. Use both equations to find two unknown speeds.
Elastic collision, target at rest: \(\begin{aligned}v_{1,f} &= \tfrac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_{1,i}\\ v_{2,f} &= \tfrac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_{1,i}\end{aligned}\) | Head on, with object 2 starting at rest. For equal masses, object 1 stops and object 2 leaves with its speed.

## EQUATIONS 9.5
* Momentum in two dimensions: \(\begin{aligned}\textstyle\sum p_{x,i} &= \textstyle\sum p_{x,f}\\ \textstyle\sum p_{y,i} &= \textstyle\sum p_{y,f}\end{aligned}\) | Momentum is conserved separately in the x and y directions. Work each direction on its own, then combine.
* Equal masses, elastic, one at rest: \(\theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ\) | The two objects leave at right angles to each other. This is why pool balls split at 90°.

## EQUATIONS 9.6
* Center of mass: \(\vec r_\text{CM} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_j m_j\vec r_j\) | The center of mass position (m) is the mass weighted average of the positions. \(M\) (kg) is the total mass.
Velocity of the center of mass: \(\vec v_\text{CM} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_j m_j\vec v_j = \frac{\vec p_\text{tot}}{M}\) | The center of mass moves at the total momentum divided by the total mass.
* Newton's second law for a system: \(\vec F_\text{ext} = M\vec a_\text{CM}\) | Only external forces (N) change how the center of mass moves. Internal forces, even an explosion, cannot.
Center of mass of a continuous object: \(\vec r_\text{CM} = \frac{1}{M}\int\vec r\,dm\) | For a smooth object, add up position times each small piece of mass \(dm\), then divide by the total mass.

## EQUATIONS 9.7
* Rocket equation: \(\Delta v = u\ln\dfrac{m_i}{m}\) | Speed gained \(\Delta v\) (m/s) from exhaust speed \(u\) (m/s, relative to the rocket) and the ratio of starting mass \(m_i\) to current mass \(m\).

## QUIZ 9.1
Q: A bowling ball and a tennis ball roll at the same speed. Which has more momentum?
A) The bowling ball
B) The tennis ball
C) They have the same momentum
D) Neither; rolling balls have no momentum
Answer: A
Why: \(p = mv\). At the same speed, the larger mass has the larger momentum.

## QUIZ 9.1
Q: A car drives around a curve at a steady speed. Does its momentum change?
A) No, because its speed is constant
B) Yes, because its direction changes
C) No, because no force acts on it
D) Yes, because its mass changes
Answer: B
Why: Momentum is a vector, \(\vec p = m\vec v\). Turning changes the direction of \(\vec v\), so \(\vec p\) changes even though the speed doesn't.

## QUIZ 9.1
Q: Which unit is the same as the unit of momentum?
A) \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)
B) \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)
C) \(\text{N/s}\)
D) \(\text{J}\)
Answer: A
Why: Mass times velocity gives \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\), which also equals \(\text{N}\cdot\text{s}\). Choice B is a newton, the unit of force.

## QUIZ 9.1
Q: Ball A has mass 1 kg and speed 4 m/s. Ball B has mass 4 kg and speed 1 m/s. Which is true?
A) Same momentum; A has more kinetic energy
B) Same momentum and same kinetic energy
C) B has more momentum; same kinetic energy
D) A has more momentum and more kinetic energy
Answer: A
Why: Both have \(p = 4\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\). But \(K = \tfrac12mv^2\) gives \(8\ \text{J}\) for A and \(2\ \text{J}\) for B, because kinetic energy grows with \(v^2\).

## QUIZ 9.1
Q: The net force on a hockey puck is zero. What happens to its momentum?
A) It stays constant
B) It slowly decreases
C) It drops to zero
D) It depends on the puck's mass
Answer: A
Why: \(\vec F_\text{net} = \frac{d\vec p}{dt}\). Zero net force means the momentum doesn't change, in size or direction.

## QUIZ 9.2
Q: Why do airbags reduce injuries in a crash?
A) They make the stop take longer, so the force is smaller
B) They reduce the person's change in momentum
C) They reduce the person's mass
D) They absorb the momentum, so it isn't conserved
Answer: A
Why: The person's change in momentum is the same either way. Since \(F_\text{ave} = \Delta p/\Delta t\), a longer stopping time means a smaller average force.

## QUIZ 9.2
Q: Two identical balls hit a wall at the same speed. A clay ball sticks; a rubber ball bounces back at the same speed. Which gets the larger impulse from the wall?
A) The rubber ball
B) The clay ball
C) They get the same impulse
D) Neither gets an impulse
Answer: A
Why: The clay ball's momentum goes from \(mv\) to \(0\). The rubber ball's goes from \(mv\) to \(-mv\), a change of \(2mv\). Impulse equals the change in momentum, so the bounce needs twice as much.

## QUIZ 9.2
Q: Which unit is the same as a newton second, the unit of impulse?
A) \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\)
B) \(\text{J}\)
C) \(\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2\)
D) \(\text{W}\)
Answer: A
Why: \(1\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s} = 1\ (\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2)(\text{s}) = 1\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}\), the unit of momentum. That fits \(\vec J = \Delta\vec p\).

## QUIZ 9.2
Q: On a graph of force against time, what does the area under the curve give?
A) The impulse
B) The work done
C) The power
D) The acceleration
Answer: A
Why: \(\vec J = \int\vec F\,dt\), which is the area under \(F\) against \(t\). Work is the area under force against position.

# ============================ 9.3 CONSERVATION OF LINEAR MOMENTUM ============================

## QUIZ 9.3
Q: When is the total momentum of a system conserved?
A) When the net external force on the system is zero
B) Only in elastic collisions
C) Only when no forces at all act on the objects
D) Only when the objects start at rest
Answer: A
Why: Forces between the objects cancel in pairs, so only outside forces can change the total. Objects can push on each other as hard as they like.

## QUIZ 9.3
Q: A heavy skater and a light skater stand at rest on ice and push each other apart. Which is true?
A) Their momenta are equal and opposite; the light one moves faster
B) The heavy one gets more momentum
C) They move apart at the same speed
D) The light one gets more momentum
Answer: A
Why: The total starts at zero and stays zero, so \(m_1v_1 = -m_2v_2\). Equal momentum sizes mean the smaller mass has the larger speed.

## QUIZ 9.3
Q: A truck and a small car collide head on. Which has the larger change in momentum?
A) They have the same size change
B) The truck
C) The car
D) It depends on their speeds
Answer: A
Why: By Newton's third law they push on each other with equal and opposite forces for the same time, so they get equal and opposite impulses. The car's velocity changes more because its mass is smaller.

## QUIZ 9.3
Q: A ball falls toward the ground. Which system has conserved momentum while it falls?
A) The ball and Earth together
B) The ball alone
C) Both the ball alone and the ball and Earth
D) Neither
Answer: A
Why: For the ball alone, gravity is an outside force, so its momentum grows. With Earth in the system, gravity is an inside pair of forces: Earth gains an equal and opposite (tiny) momentum.

## QUIZ 9.3
Q: A skater standing on ice throws a ball forward. Why is horizontal momentum conserved even though gravity acts?

B) Gravity is too small to matter
C) The ball's momentum is always zero
D) Momentum is only conserved when nothing moves
Answer: A
Why: Momentum is conserved in any direction where the outside forces add to zero. Here they are all vertical, so horizontal momentum is conserved.

# ============================ 9.4 TYPES OF COLLISIONS ============================

## QUIZ 9.4
Q: In which collisions between two objects (with no outside forces) is total momentum conserved?
A) All of them
B) Only elastic collisions
C) Only collisions where the objects stick
D) Only head on collisions
Answer: A
Why: Momentum is conserved in every collision of a closed system. What differs between collision types is kinetic energy.

## QUIZ 9.4
Q: In which kind of collision do the objects stick together?
A) Perfectly inelastic
B) Elastic
C) Explosive
D) Superelastic
Answer: A
Why: Perfectly inelastic collisions end with one shared velocity. They lose the most kinetic energy that momentum conservation allows.

## QUIZ 9.4
Q: A cue ball hits a pool ball at rest head on. Treat the balls as equal masses and the collision as elastic. What happens?
A) The cue ball stops and the other ball moves off at the cue ball's speed
B) Both balls move forward at half the speed
C) The cue ball bounces back at its starting speed
D) Both balls stop
Answer: A
Why: For equal masses in a head on elastic collision, the balls trade velocities. That keeps both momentum and kinetic energy the same. Choice B conserves momentum but loses half the kinetic energy.

## QUIZ 9.4
Q: A ball of clay hits a cart at rest and sticks to it. What happens to the momentum and the kinetic energy?
A) Momentum is conserved; some kinetic energy is lost
B) Both are conserved
C) Kinetic energy is conserved; some momentum is lost
D) Both are lost
Answer: A
Why: Momentum is always conserved in a closed collision. Sticking means the collision is perfectly inelastic, so some kinetic energy turns into thermal energy and bent clay.

# ============================ 9.5 COLLISIONS IN MULTIPLE DIMENSIONS ============================

## QUIZ 9.5
Q: In a collision in two dimensions with no outside forces, what is conserved?
A) The x and y components of total momentum, each on its own
B) Only the total speed
C) Only the x component of momentum
D) Only the size of each object's momentum
Answer: A
Why: Momentum is a vector, so each component is conserved separately. Speeds and individual momenta can change.

## QUIZ 9.5

A) 90°
B) 45°
C) 180°
D) It can be anything

## QUIZ 9.5

B) It has a north component
C) It points due east
D) It is zero

Why: The total north and south momentum started at zero, so it must stay zero. The first puck now has a north component, so the second must have an equal south component.

## QUIZ 9.5
Q: One car moving east and another moving north collide and stick. Which way does the wreck move?

B) Due east
C) Due north
D) It stops
Answer: A
Why: The wreck keeps both the east momentum of the first car and the north momentum of the second, so it moves between the two. The angle depends on which car had more momentum.

# ============================ 9.6 CENTER OF MASS ============================

## QUIZ 9.6
Q: A dumbbell has a heavy weight on one end and a light weight on the other. Where is its center of mass?
A) Closer to the heavy end
B) Exactly in the middle
C) Closer to the light end
D) Outside the dumbbell
Answer: A
Why: The center of mass is a mass weighted average of positions, so it sits closer to where more of the mass is.

## QUIZ 9.6
Q: A firework shell bursts into many pieces at the top of its path. Until the pieces land, what does their center of mass do?
A) Keeps following the shell's original parabola
B) Stops and falls straight down
C) Moves outward with the fastest piece
D) Stays at the bursting point
Answer: A
Why: The explosion forces are internal, so they can't change the motion of the center of mass. Only gravity, an outside force, acts, just as before the burst.

## QUIZ 9.6
Q: Where is the center of mass of a thin, uniform ring?
A) At the center of the ring, where there is no mass
B) Somewhere on the ring itself
C) It has no center of mass
D) At the top of the ring
Answer: A
Why: By symmetry every bit of ring has a matching bit on the opposite side, so the average position is the center. The center of mass doesn't have to be inside the object.

# ============================ 9.7 ROCKET PROPULSION ============================

## QUIZ 9.7
Q: How does a rocket speed up in empty space, with no air to push against?
A) It pushes exhaust backward, and the exhaust pushes it forward

## QUIZ 9.7

## QUIZ 9.7

B) It decreases, because fuel runs out
