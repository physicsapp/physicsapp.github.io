## SUMMARY 11.1
In short: A wheel rolling without slipping turns and moves forward together: \(v_\text{CM} = R\omega\), \(a_\text{CM} = R\alpha\), and the center moves \(d_\text{CM} = R\theta\). The point touching the ground is momentarily at rest, and the top moves at \(2v_\text{CM}\). Static friction at the contact point makes the object spin up but does no work, so mechanical energy is conserved: \(K = \frac12mv_\text{CM}^2 + \frac12I_\text{CM}\omega^2\). Down a ramp, objects with less of their mass far from the axis (smaller \(I/mr^2\)) speed up faster: a solid sphere beats a solid cylinder, which beats a hoop.
Watch out: A rolling object reaches the bottom slower than a block sliding on a frictionless ramp, because part of its energy goes into spinning.

## SUMMARY 11.2
In short: A particle with momentum \(\vec p\) at position \(\vec r\) has angular momentum \(\vec l = \vec r\times\vec p\), size \(l = rmv\sin\theta\) (kg·m²/s), pointing along the axis by the right hand rule. Even a particle moving in a straight line has angular momentum about a point off its path. A net torque changes angular momentum: \(\frac{d\vec L}{dt} = \sum\vec\tau\), the rotational version of \(\frac{d\vec p}{dt} = \sum\vec F\). A rigid body spinning about a fixed axis has \(L = I\omega\).
Watch out: Angular momentum depends on the point you measure from. A particle moving straight toward that point has \(l = 0\).

## SUMMARY 11.3
In short: If the net external torque on a system is zero, its total angular momentum stays the same: \(I_\text{i}\omega_\text{i} = I_\text{f}\omega_\text{f}\). A skater who pulls in her arms makes \(I\) smaller, so \(\omega\) grows. A diver tucks to spin faster and opens up to slow down. When two objects grab each other while turning, like a disk dropped onto a spinning turntable, angular momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is lost.
Watch out: Conserving \(L\) does not mean conserving kinetic energy. Since \(K = \frac{L^2}{2I}\), pulling in the arms raises \(K\); the skater's muscles do that work.

## SUMMARY 11.4
In short: A spinning top or gyroscope doesn't simply fall over. Gravity acting at the center of mass gives a torque \(\tau = rMg\) that is horizontal and at right angles to the spin angular momentum \(\vec L\). Since \(d\vec L = \vec\tau\,dt\), the torque changes the direction of \(\vec L\), not its size, so the axis swings around in a circle. This is precession, with angular velocity \(\omega_P = \frac{rMg}{I\omega}\). A faster spin means slower precession. Earth's axis precesses too, once about every 26,000 years.
Watch out: The precession rate is \(\omega_P\), not the spin rate \(\omega\). They appear in opposite places in \(\omega_P = \frac{rMg}{I\omega}\).

## EQUATIONS 11.1
* Rolling without slipping: \(v_\text{CM} = R\omega,\quad a_\text{CM} = R\alpha\) | A wheel of radius \(R\) (m) turning at \(\omega\) (rad/s) moves its center at \(v_\text{CM}\) (m/s); the same link holds for accelerations.
Distance rolled: \(d_\text{CM} = R\theta\) | The center moves \(d_\text{CM}\) (m) while the wheel turns through \(\theta\) (rad). One turn moves it \(2\pi R\).
* Kinetic energy of rolling: \(K = \tfrac12mv_\text{CM}^2 + \tfrac12I_\text{CM}\omega^2\) | Total kinetic energy (J) of a rolling object: moving plus spinning, each about the center of mass.
* Rolling down a ramp: \(a_\text{CM} = \dfrac{mg\sin\theta}{m + I_\text{CM}/r^2}\) | Acceleration (m/s²) of an object of mass \(m\) (kg) rolling without slipping down a ramp at angle \(\theta\). For a solid cylinder it is \(\tfrac23g\sin\theta\).
Speed after rolling down a height: \(v_\text{CM} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2gh}{1 + I_\text{CM}/mr^2}}\) | From energy conservation, starting at rest and dropping height \(h\) (m). With no rotation it would be \(\sqrt{2gh}\).

## EQUATIONS 11.2
* Angular momentum of a particle: \(\vec l = \vec r\times\vec p\) | Angular momentum (kg·m²/s) of a particle with momentum \(\vec p\) at position \(\vec r\) from the chosen point. It points along the axis by the right hand rule.
* Size of angular momentum: \(l = rmv\sin\theta\) | \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\vec r\) and \(\vec v\). For motion in a circle of radius \(r\), \(l = mvr\).
* Torque changes angular momentum: \(\dfrac{d\vec L}{dt} = \sum\vec\tau\) | The net external torque (N·m) is the rate of change of angular momentum, like \(\frac{d\vec p}{dt} = \sum\vec F\).
System of particles: \(\vec L = \vec l_1 + \vec l_2 + \cdots + \vec l_N\) | Add each particle's angular momentum, all about the same point.
* Rigid body: \(L = I\omega\) | Angular momentum (kg·m²/s) of a body with moment of inertia \(I\) (kg·m²) spinning at \(\omega\) (rad/s) about a fixed axis.

## EQUATIONS 11.3
Conservation of angular momentum: \(\dfrac{d\vec L}{dt} = 0\) | With no net external torque, the total angular momentum of a system stays the same.
* Before and after: \(I_\text{i}\omega_\text{i} = I_\text{f}\omega_\text{f}\) | When the moment of inertia changes from \(I_\text{i}\) to \(I_\text{f}\) (kg·m²) with no outside torque, \(\omega\) changes the opposite way.
Kinetic energy in terms of L: \(K = \dfrac{L^2}{2I}\) | With \(L\) fixed, a smaller \(I\) means more kinetic energy (J). The extra energy comes from work done inside the system.

## EQUATIONS 11.4
Torque from gravity on a gyroscope: \(\tau = rMg\) | A gyroscope of mass \(M\) (kg) with its center of mass \(r\) (m) from the pivot, axle horizontal. The torque is at right angles to \(\vec L\).
* Precession: \(\omega_P = \dfrac{rMg}{I\omega}\) | Precession angular velocity (rad/s) of a gyroscope spinning at \(\omega\) (rad/s) with moment of inertia \(I\) (kg·m²). Faster spin, slower precession.

# ============================ 11.1 ROLLING MOTION ============================

## QUIZ 11.1
Q: A wheel rolls without slipping. How fast is the point touching the ground moving at that instant?
A) It is momentarily at rest
B) \(v_\text{CM}\)
C) \(2v_\text{CM}\)
D) \(R\omega^2\)
Answer: A
Why: The contact point moves backward at \(R\omega\) relative to the center while the center moves forward at \(v_\text{CM} = R\omega\). They cancel, so the contact point is at rest for an instant.

## QUIZ 11.1
Q: A wheel rolls without slipping at speed \(v_\text{CM}\). How fast does the top of the wheel move?
A) \(2v_\text{CM}\)
B) \(v_\text{CM}\)
C) Zero
D) \(\frac12v_\text{CM}\)
Answer: A
Why: The top moves forward at \(R\omega = v_\text{CM}\) relative to the center, plus the center's own \(v_\text{CM}\), for \(2v_\text{CM}\) in all.

## QUIZ 11.1
Q: A solid sphere, a solid cylinder, and a hoop, all with the same mass and radius, roll from rest down the same ramp. Which reaches the bottom first?
A) The solid sphere
B) The solid cylinder
C) The hoop
D) They tie
Answer: A
Why: The sphere has the smallest \(I/mr^2\) (\(\frac25\), against \(\frac12\) and 1), so less of its energy goes into spinning and it gets the biggest acceleration.

## QUIZ 11.1
Q: A ball rolls without slipping down a ramp. Why is its mechanical energy conserved even though there is friction?
A) Static friction acts at a point that isn't moving, so it does no work
B) Friction is too small to matter
C) Kinetic friction cancels static friction
D) Mechanical energy isn't conserved
Answer: A
Why: In rolling without slipping the contact point is momentarily at rest. Static friction makes the ball spin up but takes no energy away.

## QUIZ 11.1
Q: A block slides down a frictionless ramp and a solid cylinder rolls without slipping down an identical ramp, both starting from rest at the same height. Which is faster at the bottom?
A) The block
B) The cylinder
C) Same speed
D) It depends on the mass
Answer: A
Why: Both turn the same \(mgh\) into kinetic energy, but the cylinder puts part of it into spinning, so it moves forward more slowly.

## QUIZ 11.2
Q: A puck slides in a straight line that passes 2 m from point O, with no forces on it. What is true of its angular momentum about O?
A) It is not zero and stays constant
B) It is zero, since the puck isn't turning
C) It grows as the puck gets closer to O
D) It is zero except at the closest point
Answer: A
Why: \(l = rmv\sin\theta = mvr_\perp\), and the perpendicular distance \(r_\perp\) = 2 m stays the same along the whole line. No force means no torque, so \(l\) doesn't change.

## QUIZ 11.2
Q: What are the SI units of angular momentum?
A) \(\text{kg·m}^2\text{/s}\)
B) \(\text{kg·m/s}\)
C) \(\text{N·m}\)
D) \(\text{kg·m}^2\)
Answer: A
Why: \(l = rmv\) has units m × kg × m/s. Choice B is linear momentum, C is torque, and D is moment of inertia.

## QUIZ 11.2
Q: Seen from above, a merry go round turns counterclockwise. Which way does its angular momentum point?
A) Straight up
B) Straight down
C) Along the edge, in the direction of motion
D) Toward the center
Answer: A
Why: Curl the fingers of your right hand counterclockwise; your thumb points up. That is the direction of \(\vec L\).

## QUIZ 11.2
Q: What changes the angular momentum of a system?
A) A net external torque
B) Any net force
C) A change in the moment of inertia
D) Nothing; it never changes
Answer: A
Why: \(\frac{d\vec L}{dt} = \sum\vec\tau\). A force through the axis gives no torque and leaves \(L\) alone; changing \(I\) with no torque changes \(\omega\) instead.

## QUIZ 11.2
Q: A ball moves straight toward point O. What is its angular momentum about O?
A) Zero
B) \(mvr\)
C) It depends on how fast it is going
D) It increases as it gets closer
Answer: A
Why: \(\vec r\) and \(\vec v\) point along the same line, so \(\theta = 180^\circ\) and \(\sin\theta = 0\). Then \(l = rmv\sin\theta = 0\).

# ============================ 11.3 CONSERVATION OF ANGULAR MOMENTUM ============================

## QUIZ 11.3
Q: A spinning skater pulls in her arms. What happens?
A) She spins faster; her angular momentum stays the same
B) She spins faster; her angular momentum increases
C) She spins slower; her angular momentum stays the same
D) Nothing changes
Answer: A
Why: No outside torque acts, so \(L = I\omega\) is constant. Pulling in her arms lowers \(I\), so \(\omega\) goes up.

## QUIZ 11.3
Q: When the skater pulls in her arms, what happens to her rotational kinetic energy?
A) It increases; her muscles do work pulling her arms in
B) It stays the same, like her angular momentum
C) It decreases
D) It becomes zero
Answer: A
Why: \(K = \frac{L^2}{2I}\). With \(L\) fixed and \(I\) smaller, \(K\) is larger. The energy comes from the work her arms do.

## QUIZ 11.3
Q: Why does a diver pull into a tuck after leaving the board?
A) To make her moment of inertia smaller, so she spins faster
B) To make her angular momentum larger
C) To fall faster
D) To make a torque on herself
Answer: A
Why: Once she leaves the board, her angular momentum about her center of mass stays the same. Tucking lowers \(I\) and raises \(\omega\); opening up slows the spin for entry.

## QUIZ 11.3
Q: A disk is dropped onto a spinning turntable and the two then turn together. Which is true?
A) Angular momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is lost
B) Both are conserved
C) Kinetic energy is conserved; angular momentum is not
D) Neither is conserved
Answer: A
Why: Friction between them is internal, so there is no outside torque and \(L\) is conserved. Like a perfectly inelastic collision, the two end up moving together and kinetic energy turns into heat.

## QUIZ 11.3
Q: When is the angular momentum of a system conserved?
A) When the net external torque on it is zero
B) Only when no forces act on it
C) Only when its moment of inertia stays the same
D) Only when it isn't moving
Answer: A
Why: \(\frac{d\vec L}{dt} = \sum\vec\tau_\text{ext}\). Forces may act, as long as their torques add to zero, for example forces through the axis.

# ============================ 11.4 PRECESSION OF A GYROSCOPE ============================

## QUIZ 11.4
Q: A spinning gyroscope rests with one end of its axle on a stand. Why doesn't it fall over?
A) Gravity's torque changes the direction of \(\vec L\), so the axle swings around instead of falling
B) Spinning cancels gravity
C) There is no torque on it
D) The stand pushes up exactly at the center of mass
Answer: A
Why: The torque from gravity is horizontal and at right angles to \(\vec L\). Since \(d\vec L = \vec\tau\,dt\), \(\vec L\) turns sideways rather than tipping down.

## QUIZ 11.4
Q: A gyroscope is spun up to twice the spin rate. What happens to its precession?
A) It precesses half as fast
B) It precesses twice as fast
C) It precesses at the same rate
D) It stops precessing
Answer: A
Why: \(\omega_P = \frac{rMg}{I\omega}\), so doubling \(\omega\) halves \(\omega_P\).

## QUIZ 11.4
Q: For a gyroscope with a horizontal axle, how does the torque from gravity compare to its spin angular momentum \(\vec L\)?
A) Horizontal and at right angles to \(\vec L\)
B) Parallel to \(\vec L\)
C) Pointing straight down
D) Opposite to \(\vec L\)
Answer: A
Why: \(\vec\tau = \vec r\times M\vec g\), with \(\vec r\) along the axle and \(\vec g\) down, is horizontal and at right angles to the axle, so it only changes the direction of \(\vec L\).

## QUIZ 11.4
Q: Earth's spin axis precesses. About how long does one full precession take?
A) About 26,000 years
B) One day
C) One year
D) About 26 years
Answer: A
Why: Torques from the Sun and Moon on Earth's bulge at the equator make the axis sweep around very slowly, about once every 26,000 years, because Earth's spin angular momentum is so large.
